# Georgiadis Photography

Wildlife of Central Florida: a gallery of the Finals from the Nikon archive.

The site is static (GitHub Pages) and rebuilds itself:

1. **Google Drive.** `drive-sync/PublicGallerySync.gs` runs every 30 minutes in Google Apps Script. It copies every Finals photo from the Nikon folder into `Nikon/Public Gallery` (shared as "anyone with the link"), keeps the copies in step with the originals, and writes `manifest.json` there. RAW folders are never touched.
2. **The cloud job.** A scheduled job reads the manifest and the private Workshop's library (species IDs, ratings, hidden photos, notes) straight from the Workshop's database (`tools/workshop_library.py local` checks the dump), sorts new photos itself, adds camera data for new photos, and runs `tools/build_site.py`, which writes `data/gallery.json`, `index.html`, the link-preview pages and the calls of any new species here. It runs every six hours in one long-lived cloud session, the only one set up to write to the Workshop and push here. The Workshop's **Publish now** (and **Make previews now**) reads the job's instructions and schedules one extra run of them into that same session, about a minute and a half ahead. Firing the job directly would start a bare session that can do neither.
3. **The site.** `index.html` loads `core/` (the shared gallery engine) and `site.js`, which shows the public copies straight from Google Drive.

**Getting around.** Four sections: **Gallery** (the grid, or the Universe: a switch on both), **Exhibitions**, **Species** (four tabs, each with its own ways of looking: **Guide**, by family or A–Z; **Life list**, as a list, the tree of life or the night sky; **Calls**, every call or a day in the marsh; **Cabinet & plates**, the cabinet or the natural-history plates) and **Outings** (the list, the field notebook and the archive in numbers, as tabs). The header carries them with the live sky, the search, Play and one menu; a page inside a section lights that section. The menu holds the display settings (Auto, Light or Dark; Open bursts; Depth), the sound settings (Bird calls, Soundscape), your favourites and a few actions (Play an exhibition, Surprise me, Email dispatches, Keyboard shortcuts). On a phone or an upright tablet the header is one slim row that tucks away as you scroll, and Home and the four sections sit in a tab bar at the foot of the screen. A link opens its page at the top; Back and Forward return to the place you left on a page (each history entry the site draws is marked, since browsers report a tapped link and a Back the same way). Old Journal links still work: they go to the field notebook (or, for the tree, to the Life list's tree).

**One search.** The header's search (or `/`, or Ctrl/Cmd+K, on any page and in the Workshop) opens a panel that answers as you type: species (with their photo counts), outings (by title, date or the species seen), exhibitions, photographs (a strip of the best matches with the words it understood, as on the Gallery: "herons in flight" is a family and a behaviour, and the whole set opens in the Gallery) and the pages of the site ("night sky", "plates", "notebook"). Whatever is named exactly comes first; photographs lead when the words ask for qualities of a photograph (a light, what a bird is doing, a colour, a month); otherwise species, photographs, outings, exhibitions and pages, in that order. Arrow keys and Enter, or a tap, open an answer; Esc, Cancel or Back closes the panel, and Back after opening an answer returns to the page the search was made from. With nothing typed it offers recent searches (kept on this device) and a few to try. On a phone it fills the screen. The Gallery keeps its own box and Filters panel for narrowing the photographs in front of you.

**Every page is laid out the same way.** A header comes first: a small kicker, the title, one line of intro and, on the right, the page's tabs. Tabs are one control everywhere (the Species tabs, the Outings views, Grid or Universe). Where a tab has more than one way of looking, a smaller row under it picks one (under Life list: List, Tree, Night sky), and the tab stays lit on each. Within a section the title stays the same from tab to tab, so only the intro changes and the tabs never move. On a phone the tabs sit under the title; the four Species tabs share the width evenly, and longer rows scroll sideways with the current tab in view. Filters stay chips, and only pages that filter have a slim toolbar that stays in reach as you scroll: the Gallery, and the Species guide when a breadcrumb has narrowed it to one family ("Showing Herons, Egrets & Bitterns ×, 11 of 122 species"). A page inside a section shows the way back up as its kicker, e.g. "Species › Alligators & Caimans · Crocodilia · Life list No. 14". The family there links to the Species guide, narrowed to that family. Every such page ends the same way: picture cards for the previous and next species, outing, exhibition, plate or story, then a link back to the whole section. The pieces are `pageHeader`, `tabs`, `crumbs` and `pageFoot` in `core/app.js`, shared with the modules and the Workshop through `GP.app.helpers`.

## What's in the gallery

- **Home**: a shutter opening on a first visit, then the strongest frames, each one moving with its depth (or playing its burst), with the headline set in the corner the bird leaves empty. Below, six sections: **From the field** (the latest outing as a dispatch, the two before it and the way to the whole field diary), **Highlights**, **Right now** (three cards: the frame made nearest this time of day, who's around this week of the year on a year wheel, and the species of the day with its call), the exhibitions, and **Other ways round** (small doors to the Universe, the cabinet, a day in the marsh, the night sky, the plates, the tree of life and the calls). Returning visitors also see their own archive (new since last visit, favourites, where they left off), and "On this day" appears as a strip when there is one. On a phone the Right now cards and the doors are swiped sideways.
- **Exhibitions** (`#/rooms`): themed rooms (and any curated collections) hung like a gallery wall, with a label beside every print, then the rest of the room as a grid. On a phone the rooms after the first sit two to a row, each with Play on its cover. Each room, outing, species page or search can be played full screen as an exhibition: labels, speed, the birds' calls, keyboard and swipe.
- **Gallery**: by month, newest first, with a small card where each outing's day begins; on a phone two or three photographs to a row. A date scrubber down the right edge (shown while the page moves, or with the pointer at the edge) jumps to any month. One Filters button opens a panel (a sheet on a phone) for type, rating, light, what the animal is doing, colour, month, year, order and every frame; the filters in force show as chips above the results, and live in the address so a filtered gallery can be shared. Search understands species, families, light, months and colours as you type. Long grids draw a screenful at a time, the rest while the page is idle.
- **Species, Outings**: density and "new since your last visit"; the field guide (a photo grid by family, the cabinet a tap away; families of one or two species share rows, each under a small label, and on a phone the captive and unidentified show six, then a button for the rest) with calls, light and "seen with"; each outing's day placed by the minute against that day's light, with "Hear the day"; charts, records and the tree of life. A species or outing page leads with its photographs: the title, the note and the actions, then the grid. Its field notes (the numbers, when in the year, what it was doing, the light, what it was seen with; for an outing, its species and the day in colour) sit in a column beside the photographs on a wide screen, under them on a tablet, and in a row of cards to swipe on a phone. Every light and the outing's day line follow. On a phone the species page doesn't repeat its best photograph above the grid that opens with it, and its photographs start on the first screen: the call is the first field-notes card under them, and Play, the plate and Share share one row. In the Outings list each outing's photograph is a slideshow of its strongest frames, a different species first: swipe it (or use its arrows with a mouse) for the next, tap a photograph to open it large among them, and the last slide leads into the whole day. The words beside it open the outing; on a phone they sit under the photograph beside the date, with the note kept to two lines.
- **Bursts**: a burst shows as one tile with its frame count. Run a mouse across it to flick through its frames. On a phone or tablet, each swipe across it moves on one frame: left for the next, right for the one before, round and round (the frame follows the finger a little, and the page still scrolls up and down under it). Clicking or tapping opens the frame the tile is showing. In the viewer, left and right step through every frame of a burst before moving on.
- **Getting around in the viewer**: along the foot runs a filmstrip of every photograph in the set it was opened from (a gallery, an outing, a room, a search), the current one lit and centred, with "3 of 168" beside the burst's frames. A tap or click on the strip goes straight to that photograph; it scrolls under a finger or a trackpad, and in a long set it draws more as it nears either end. Home and End go to the first and last. The photograph fits in the room above the strip. On a phone, swipe a photograph down to close it: it shrinks and follows the finger as the page shows through behind, and a short pull springs back. The title has a line of its own under the buttons on a phone. The controls fade after a moment (a tap on the photograph brings them back), stay while you're using them, and once faded don't take taps.
- **Loading in the viewer**: a photograph never opens or slides in blank. The sharpest copy already on the device shows at once (its tile, a print, or its soft placeholder), then a quick preview, then a copy sized to the photograph as it shows on this screen (its box at the screen's pixel density, so an upright photograph isn't fetched at the width of the whole screen), each swapped in only once it has fully arrived and been decoded. On a sharp (high-density) screen a copy at one pixel a point comes in between, a quarter of the size, so the photograph looks clean within a moment. The photograph the viewer opens on starts its sharp copy at once, ahead of everything else; when stepping, it starts once a photograph has been looked at for a moment, not for each one flicked past. The neighbours' previews are fetched straight away at low priority, and their sharp copies once the current one is sharp (not on a slow connection or with Data Saver on). The filmstrip, a burst's frames and the details panel's pictures wait until the photograph is sharp (the panel's until it is open), so they never hold it up. On the site the preview is Google's small copy of the public photograph and the original loads when you zoom in; in the Workshop the preview is the one the Workshop made, and the original (where the Drive connector can hand it over) replaces it by itself.
- **Favourites**: the heart in the viewer (or L) hangs a photograph in the visitor's own room. Favourites, "new since last visit" and the daily score stay on the visitor's device.
- **Universe** (`#/universe`, the Gallery's second view): every photograph as a tile on one canvas, rearranged into a colour wheel, a skyline of outings, a 24-hour dial or clusters by family. Zoom far into a tile to open it.
- **Viewfinder and loupe**: with a mouse, the cursor over a photograph becomes a small reticle with the exposure under it, green over the subject. Hold Shift (or long-press on a phone) for a loupe that loads the original as you magnify.
- **Cabinet of specimens**: the species guide as a cabinet, one drawer per family, with museum labels.
- **Tree of life** (Species, Life list): the life list as a radial tree that grows in the order species were first photographed.
- **Bird calls**: each species' call on its page and in the viewer, off until the visitor turns sound on. Recordings come from iNaturalist under Creative Commons licences and are credited beside every player.
- **The live sky**: the header is the sky over Central Florida right now (sun, twilight, night) with the moon in its true phase; click it for sunrise, sunset and what's next. Every page takes on the colours of the photograph in front of it.
- **Living photographs**: covers and large photographs arrive like a lens finding focus (grid tiles fade up over their soft preview), outing pages develop like prints in a tray, backlit frames carry a light leak, and bursts step frame by frame (move the pointer across a burst in the gallery; in the viewer, the frame arrows or up and down, and V plays it). Open bursts in the menu (or X) opens every burst on any page into a tile per frame, dealt out from where its burst sat, and groups them again; grouped is the default each visit. With **Depth** turned on (in the menu, or M; off by default) each subject stands a little in front of its background as the pointer moves or the phone tilts, from depth maps (below).
- **How it was shot** (viewer, H): the aperture closing to its f-number, the shutter's slit and how far a bird flying past would have moved, the field of view, the grain of the ISO, and the sky that moment: where the sun and moon were.
- **Species pages**: a watercolour wash in the bird's own colours, the name sings with its call, "In every light" (the species from first light to dusk) and its natural-history plate.
- **Plates** (`#/plates`): one plate per family in the manner of an old natural history, numbered, with oval vignettes and a key; printable.
- **Night sky** (`#/sky`): every species a star, its brightness the number of photographs, families as constellations.
- **A day in the marsh** (`#/day`): sixty seconds from 4 am to night, each bird calling from its side of the marsh at the hour it's most often photographed.
- **Soundscape** (menu, S): the place each photograph was made (water, pines, surf, open ground, crickets after dark), with the birds calling as you pass them. Off until the visitor turns it on.
- **Exhibition rooms**: the camera follows the bird and focus pulls between slides in the player; B turns the lights down in a room and each print gets its own lamp.
- **The field notebook** (Outings): the year as a colour barcode, then month spreads with handwritten lines, taped prints and new species, inked as they turn into view. It opens on the latest four months; "Turn back further" opens the rest and notes it in the address (`&all=1`), so Back from a species or an outing returns to every month, at the place you left. Every outing can also be read as a story (`#/story/<outing>`): the sky moving with the clock, each bird arriving in turn, with sound.
- **Hover previews**: with a mouse, rest on a link to a species, outing, room or plate for a card with its photograph.
- **Dispatches by email** (`#/newsletter`): a short email whenever a new outing goes up, with its best photograph and a link straight to the outing. Offered once to each visitor in a small card, and kept in the menu and the footer. Run by a Google Apps Script; see [newsletter/README.md](newsletter/README.md) to set it up.
- **Link previews**: shared links (`p/<photo>`, `s/<species>`, `o/<outing>`, `r/<room>`) are small pages whose previews show that exact photograph, then open it in the gallery.

## Bird calls

`tools/fetch_calls.py --repo .` looks up research-grade, openly licensed recordings for each species on iNaturalist, keeps the clearest of the top candidates (judged in the frequency band where the species calls), and saves a trimmed, filtered, loudness-matched 10-second clip with a sonogram and its credit. Species with no usable recording are remembered so they aren't retried; `--retry-none` tries them again and `--sonograms` redraws the sonograms. The build runs it for new species automatically when ffmpeg is installed.

## Depth maps

`tools/depth_maps.py --repo .` estimates depth for each public photograph with Depth Anything V2 Small (Apache-2.0, run on the CPU with onnxruntime) and saves a 192-pixel greyscale map in `depth/`, listed in `depth/index.json`. The site uses them for the Depth setting's gentle 2.5D movement, to keep ripples off the subject and to place the home page's headline clear of the bird. Existing maps are kept, maps of photographs no longer public are removed, and `--limit` caps how many are made in one run (the build spends at most 90 seconds a run on them and makes the rest next time, installing onnxruntime if it is missing; `--no-depth` skips it). The model (about 100 MB) is downloaded once to `~/.cache/georgiadis/` or `$GP_MODEL_CACHE`. Without a map a photograph simply doesn't move.

GDRIVE_SA_KEY=... python3 tools/build_site.py build --db <database dump> --repo . --dev
